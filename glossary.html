Brand Protection Glossary

Plain-language definitions of the terms that come up when brands defend themselves online — from copycat apps to trade dress.

BCDEFHIKLMRTV
Brand Abuse
Any unauthorized use of a company's brand assets — name, logo, interface, or identity — intended to deceive customers or divert revenue. It spans copycat apps, impersonation accounts, phishing domains, and marketplace fraud.
Brand Intelligence
The practice of synthesizing raw detection data into prioritized, verified, actionable insight. Where monitoring flags mentions, brand intelligence determines which represent genuine threats, ranks them by business impact, and routes them to enforcement.
Brand Monitoring
Automated scanning that alerts a company whenever its keywords, logo, or brand elements appear online. Monitoring produces raw signal; it does not verify, prioritize, or act.
Copycat App
An application published by an unauthorized third party that imitates a legitimate app's name, icon, screenshots, description, or functionality in order to divert users searching for the original product.
Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
The total cost of acquiring one paying customer. Brand abuse inflates CAC when infringers bid on branded keywords or outrank the legitimate brand, forcing higher ad spend to defend terms the brand already owns.
DMCA Notice
A formal takedown request issued under the Digital Millennium Copyright Act, asserting that content infringes a copyright. Issuing an inaccurate or bad-faith DMCA notice can expose the sender to liability for misrepresentation.
Enforcement
The operational process of removing infringing assets: verifying the violation, assembling evidence, filing through the correct platform channel, and pursuing escalation until the case closes. Distinct from detection, which only identifies threats.
Escalation
Advancing a stalled or rejected complaint beyond first-level review — through resubmission with strengthened evidence, higher-tier support channels, or parallel enforcement on other platforms where the same actor operates.
Executive Impersonation
The creation of fake profiles impersonating a company's leadership on social platforms, typically to authorize fraudulent wire transfers, promote investment scams, or damage corporate reputation by posting false statements.
False Positive
A flagged asset that turns out not to be infringing — an authorized reseller, a legitimate partner, a parody account, or a fan page. Filing complaints against false positives damages partnerships and erodes standing with platform moderators.
Human-in-the-Loop
An enforcement model in which a trained analyst reviews and verifies every algorithmically flagged threat before any complaint is filed. The safeguard that prevents automated tools from acting on false positives.
Impersonation
Pretending to be a company, its employees, or its executives. Distinct from infringement, which is the unauthorized use of protected brand elements. Most copycat situations involve both.
Infringement
The unauthorized use of protected intellectual property — trademarks, copyrights, or trade dress — without permission from the rights holder.
Keyword Abuse
Stuffing or bidding on a competitor's trademarked brand terms in app store listings or search advertising, in order to intercept traffic intended for the legitimate brand.
Lifetime Value (LTV)
The total revenue a customer generates over their relationship with a business. Copycat apps erode LTV when users encounter an inferior knockoff, attribute the poor experience to the legitimate brand, and churn.
Lookalike Domain
A web domain registered to closely resemble a legitimate one — through character substitution, added hyphens, or an alternate top-level domain — typically used to host phishing pages or fake login portals.
Marketplace Abuse
Unauthorized listings on e-commerce or app marketplaces that misuse a brand's identity, sell counterfeit goods, or falsely claim official affiliation with the brand.
Rogue APK Site
A third-party website distributing modified or repackaged versions of an Android application outside the official Google Play Store, often bypassing paywalls or bundling malware under the legitimate brand's name.
Takedown
The removal of an infringing asset — an app listing, social account, domain, or marketplace listing — by the platform that hosts it, in response to a substantiated enforcement request.
Trade Dress
The visual appearance and overall look and feel of a product or its packaging — including interface layout, color scheme, and iconography — which can be protected when it signals the source of the product to consumers.
Typosquatting
Registering domain names that are common misspellings or visual near-matches of a legitimate domain, in order to capture mistyped traffic or host convincing phishing pages.
Verification
The analyst review step that confirms a flagged asset constitutes genuine infringement, determines the applicable violation category, and establishes what evidence the relevant platform will act on — before any complaint is filed.
